# Caesar Principle

Status: **ADOPTED principle**; runtime enforcement is **CONCEPT**.

Give each domain what properly belongs to it: computation to the machine; meaning, agency, interpretation, responsibility, and final choice to the human. This is a division of strengths, not a hierarchy of worth or a restriction on what humans may learn or do.

| Human retains | AI may perform within granted scope |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose, values, and intended outcomes | Search, memory assistance, analysis, and coding |
| Interpretation and acceptance of tradeoffs | Simulation, comparison, and evidence organization |
| Authorization and consequential final choice | Tool execution, verification, and bounded orchestration |
| Responsibility for chosen objectives | Repetitive engineering complexity |

Delegation must be explicit enough to understand its purpose, scope, and limits. A human can authorize a bounded workflow in advance; this principle does not require a confirmation dialog for every internal step. Reaching a new consequential boundary requires the authority appropriate to that boundary.

## Product consequences

- Explain a proposed action in terms of its effect on the human's objective.
- Make evidence, alternatives, unknowns, and recovery limits inspectable.
- Preserve the ability to decline, stop future work, or select another approach. Stopping does not promise to reverse actions already completed.
- Distinguish AI recommendations from human decisions and verified outcomes.
- Offer understandable defaults without removing advanced inspection or manual control.

For example, a future runtime engine may explain that two installations were observed and propose a task-specific selection. The human sees the evidence and intended scope. The engine cannot infer permission to remove the other installation merely because it appears unused.
